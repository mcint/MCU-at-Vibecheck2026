What you're looking at: two vending machines with the same buttons but different refund rules, and a Claude (Haiku) adversary that was given only the public rules and told to walk away with both the drink and its coin. Every move — Claude's, the motor's, and the machine's reply — is executed by a compiled Lean program; nothing on screen is faked.

What's proven: for the repaired rule, accepted_repaired_history_safe is an induction over every accepted move history (no length bound) showing the customer never holds coin and drink together; for the old rule, broken_policy_violates_promise exhibits the exploit as a theorem. Axioms: propext only.

How to attack it: press ▶ Watch AI attack (replays the recorded Claude run), or press the cabinet buttons yourself — Lean rejects anything the policy disallows. Step with ← →. Caveat: it's an 11-state model written by us; the theorem is about the model, not real hardware.

Business ruleLean representation
The old rule returns the coin while delivery is still active. The repaired rule waits: a delivered drink keeps payment; a jam returns the coin.
Actual proof: repaired_policy_never_gives_both is an induction over every allowed repaired-policy transaction. accepted_repaired_history_safe connects the API’s accepted histories to that invariant. No SMT solver is used.
An unsuccessful LLM attack is only an experiment result. The separate Lean theorem is the model-relative safety guarantee.
  

  
Attack log & experiment details
Claude gets public business rules and player/scheduler actions. The known counterexample, proof labels, and winning-region hints are withheld. The machine’s actions are chosen by its Lean-defined policy.
One coin, one drink, explicit turns. No indefinite delays, retries, real money, or actual vending hardware are modeled.
